A Study on Machine Learning Applications in Healthcare

Abstract
This paper explores machine learning applications in healthcare diagnostics. We present a novel approach to early disease detection using deep neural networks trained on electronic health records.

Introduction
Machine learning has gained significant attention in recent years for its potential to revolutionize healthcare. The growing availability of electronic health records presents new opportunities for developing predictive models that can assist clinicians in making timely and accurate diagnoses.

Methods
We collected data from 500 patients over 2 years from three major hospitals. The dataset includes demographics, lab results, vital signs, and diagnostic outcomes. We employed a transformer-based architecture with attention mechanisms to identify patterns indicative of disease progression.

Results
Our model achieved 95.2% accuracy on the test set with a sensitivity of 93.8% and specificity of 96.1%. The AUC-ROC was 0.987, significantly outperforming baseline methods including logistic regression and random forests.

Discussion
The results demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach for clinical decision support. The model's interpretability features allow clinicians to understand which factors contribute most to predictions, addressing a key barrier to clinical adoption.

Conclusion
This study confirms that machine learning can improve healthcare outcomes when applied to well-structured electronic health records. Future work will focus on multi-center validation and prospective clinical trials.
